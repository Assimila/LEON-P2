Aims

This work is part of the Pilot 2 (Agrifood Supply Chains) of the LEON project (Leveraging Earth Observation for Nature Finance), funded by ESA (project website here).

The aims of this work are to:

  • 1: Derive different biodiversity indices (average abundance, similarity, and the Biodiversity Intactness Index - BII) for oil-palm plantations focusing on Pilot 2’s focal area, Indonesia and Malaysia.

  • 2: Explore the role and ability of Earth Observation data in explaining and predicting biodiversity change in the pilot region.

  • 3: Derive BII for the mill areas of interest, as a useful indicator for financial institutions.

The approach is based on the PREDICTS database Hudson et al. 2014, which is a collection of independent studies that have sampled biodiversity in-situ, in different land-use types. PREDICTS notably underpins the global calculations of the BII. In PREDICTS-based approaches, biodiversity change is inferred from a space-for-time substitution, by comparing assumed pristine locations (sites sampled in minimally-used primary vegetation) to disturbed sites (e.g. located in cropland, secondary vegetation, or plantations).

Here, we specifically investigates whether we can improve BII predictions by considering EO-derived data in the biodiversity models that underpin BII (using EO-derived data as explanatory variables for biodivrsity change). In particular, we consider the following EO-derived datasets:

  • Oil-palm plantation age. We investigate wheter plantation age influences the biodiversity indicators (abundance). For plantations at a global scale, Tudge et al. (2023) found a significant effect for species richness. Note that Tudge et al. (2023) did not detect any effect of age on abundance in oil palm plantations.

Here, plantation age is derived from Danylo et al. (2021), who produced a map of the extent and age of detection of oil palm plantations encompassing Malaysia and Indonesia.

This document thus presents the step-by-step analyses (including loading data, code, and deriving outputs) for investigating the biodiversity impacts of oil-palm plantations in Indonesia and Malaysia using the PREDICTS database.

Acknowledgements: Adriana de Palma wrote a tutorial for deriving BII using the PREDICTS database, which was a useful source in developping this work. Tim Newbold wrote useful packages for data preparation and model fitting that we employed in this work.

  • When secondary vegetation is grouped into a single category (not considering different ages/stages), including land-use intensity improves the model. By using the model that does not consider land-use intensity, we likely under-estimate average biodiversity declines in some of the most disturbed land uses, as shown in plot above.

  • However, when considering secondary vegetation stages (mature/intermediate/young), there isn’t strong evidence that including land-use intensity improves the model. We still likely under-estimate average biodiversity declines in the most disturbed (intensely-used) land uses, as shown in plot above.

  • Given that, for predictions, we don’t have layers providing intensity for the different land uses, we choose the model that distinguishes different secondary vegetation types, without considering land use intensity. Indeed, we will assume that we can approximate secondary vegetation stage (for predictions) from the EO-derived forest integrity layer. We however don’t have data at this stage on land-use intensity that we could use for prediction.

  • The abundance model we choose is therefore: ‘abundance_model_SV_treecover’.

## extracting BII values around mill areas for each mill, getting the corresponding WC and Fores integrity layers, and calculating median BII
RastValues <- function(BII_data, 
                       Forest_Integrity_data,
                       WC_data,
                       mill_location, 
                       Year){
  
  ## croppping and masking world cover data -- if the mill falls within Indonesia/Malaysia 
  Extent_inter <-
      relate(WC_data, mill_location, "intersects")
  
    if (Extent_inter) {
      
      WC_mill <- crop(WC_data, mill_location)
      WC_mill <- mask(WC_mill, mill_location)
      writeRaster(WC_mill, 
              paste0('../../results/3_validation/Land_cover/', Year, '/', 
                    mill_location$UML.ID, '.tif'),
                    overwrite=TRUE)
  
    ## croppping and masking BII data (global dataset from Gassert et al.)
  BII_mill <- crop(BII_data, mill_location)
  BII_mill <- mask(BII_mill, mill_location)
  BII_mill[BII_mill==0] <- NA
  writeRaster(BII_mill, 
              paste0('../../results/3_validation/BII_Gassert/', Year, '/', 
                    mill_location$UML.ID, '.tif'),
                    overwrite=TRUE)
  
  ## croppping and masking forest intergrity data
  FI_mill <- crop(Forest_Integrity_data, mill_location)
  FI_mill <- mask(FI_mill, mill_location)
  FI_mill[FI_mill<0] <- NA
  writeRaster(FI_mill, 
              paste0('../../results/3_validation/Forest_integrity/', Year, '/', 
                    mill_location$UML.ID, '.tif'),
                    overwrite=TRUE)
  
  ## median BII value from Gassert 2026
  medianVal <- median(values(BII_mill), na.rm=TRUE)
  sumVal <- sum(values(BII_mill), na.rm=TRUE)
  Vals <- data.frame(Median_BII=medianVal, Sum_BII=sumVal, UML.ID=mill_location$UML.ID)
  return(Vals)
    } else{
          print('Mill is not in Indonesia or Malaysia')
      return(data.frame(Median_BII='Mill is not in Indonesia or Malaysia', 
                        Sum_BII='Mill is not in Indonesia or Malaysia', 
                        UML.ID=mill_location$UML.ID))
      }
}

6. CONCLUSIONS

We found significant biodiversity declines in oil-palm plantations compared to primary vegetation. Total abundance declined by an average 15%, and similarity by an average 43%. We provide BII for mills for 5 companies in Indonesia and Malaysia, considering a 25km buffer area around the mills.

Our estimations improve global BII estimations by accounting for the spatial distribution of oil palm plantations, and specifically estimating the relative declines in biodiversity (abundance and similarity) in oil palm plantations compared to primary vegetation.

Challenges to think further about: - how to better align PREDICTS categories with EO-products on land use and land use intensity, including: –> how to get proxies for land use intensity –> how to better represent primary and secondary vegetation in world cover maps.
